# Analytics & Market Intelligence Foundation

## Mission Boundary

This foundation is documentation and architecture preparation only. It does not implement production features, APIs, database models, migrations, UI dashboards, analytics pipelines, tracking systems, data collection infrastructure, recommendation engines, AI systems, advertising systems, user profiling systems, payment analytics, marketplace analytics, investor dashboards, automated decisions, or production infrastructure.

## Analytics Vision

Analytics is a decision-support foundation that helps Khedmah Digital improve services, understand market needs, and plan future expansion.

Analytics purpose:

- Improve user experience.
- Understand service demand.
- Identify growth opportunities.
- Support expansion decisions.

Analytics must be aggregated, privacy-aware, Arabic-first, and governance-controlled. It must not become user surveillance, advertising optimization, paid ranking, data selling, AI recommendation, or marketplace intelligence.

## Analytics Model

The future analytics model should connect public and aggregated platform signals to decision support:

```text
Platform Activity
↓
Privacy-Aware Aggregation
↓
Service Demand Insight
↓
Geographic Opportunity
↓
Provider Network Growth
↓
Expansion Decision Support
```

### Model Principles

- Analyze patterns, not private individuals.
- Prefer aggregated counts and trends over personal histories.
- Separate operational event definitions from business decisions.
- Keep private user, provider, owner, worker, partner, verification, and payment data out of public analytics.
- Require consent, retention, access control, deletion, and audit rules before future implementation.
- Use analytics to support planning, not automated decisions about users or providers.

## User Usage Analytics Foundation

Future analytics may become compatible with understanding:

- Search activity.
- Service discovery activity.
- Category interest.
- Business profile views.
- Service page views.
- Saved/favorite services.
- Repeat interactions.

### User Usage Principles

- Do not expose personal user behavior.
- Use aggregated and privacy-aware analytics principles.
- Do not create user profiling systems.
- Do not create personal tracking systems.
- Do not publish individual user journeys, favorites, saved services, or repeat interactions.
- Do not sell user data.

## Geographic Intelligence

Future geographic intelligence should use structured location analysis:

```text
Country
↓
City
↓
Area
↓
Service Coverage
```

Future location analysis may include:

- Service availability by area.
- Provider density.
- User demand by location.
- Expansion opportunities.

### Geographic Principles

- Avoid free-text-only location analytics.
- Use country, city, area, and service coverage as governed values.
- Avoid precise-location exposure unless future consent and privacy contracts approve it.
- Publish only aggregate geographic insights.
- Keep private addresses, personal locations, worker routes, and job locations out of public analytics.

## Architecture Decisions

1. Define analytics as decision support, not user surveillance or automated decision-making.
2. Use aggregated, privacy-aware insights as the default analytics posture.
3. Separate usage analytics, search intelligence, service demand intelligence, geographic intelligence, provider network analytics, sharing analytics compatibility, Job Work analytics compatibility, and investor intelligence.
4. Keep analytics compatible with public discovery, sharing, Job Work, partner network, trust, taxonomy, and location foundations without implementing new runtime systems.
5. Prevent analytics from becoming AI recommendations, advertising, ranking, paid visibility, marketplace analytics, payment analytics, user profiling, data selling, or automated decisions.
6. Preserve Arabic-first and RTL expectations for future analytics presentation.

## Risks

- Usage analytics could become personal tracking if aggregation and consent boundaries are weak.
- Search intelligence could reveal sensitive individual demand if query data is too granular.
- Geographic intelligence could expose private locations if area aggregation is too small.
- Provider analytics could become ranking, advertising, or paid visibility without strict governance.
- Sharing analytics could drift into social analytics if reactions, followers, or social graphs are introduced.
- Job Work analytics could expose worker performance or job location data without privacy contracts.
- Investor intelligence could overexpose sensitive growth or financial information if not aggregate and governance-controlled.
- Analytics could become automated decisioning or AI recommendations if future boundaries are not preserved.
